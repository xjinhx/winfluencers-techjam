# PRD — merchandising facets: audience gating, facet-first ordering, within-cell popularity

**Status:** Phase 0 measured; **T1a BUILT, VERIFIED, and live.** T1b and T2 killed on
evidence. T3 remains optional with measured public-set upside ~0.
**Date:** 2026-08-31 · **Build:** `eba9e70`, `TechnicalScore = 0.909328`, HR@10 = 1.000
**Origin:** He Jinhong, from `public_0199` — *"what are some marketing persuasion
tactics used in the real world to differentiate between the 2 products... i want to
implement that in the agent... without asking even more questions"*, then
*"it needs to be a generalised solution"* (private 800, not the public 200).

---

## 0. Summary

Three real merchandising tactics were proposed, each mapping to a computable feature:

| | tactic | retail rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | **audience gating** | no retailer cross-merchandises men's against boys' |
| **T2** | **facet-first ordering** | faceted search sorts *within* a facet bucket; a bestseller badge never crosses a facet boundary |
| **T3** | **within-cell popularity** | social proof is normalised within a department, never site-wide |

All three are variations on one mechanism: **use a facet to partition the pool, then
let relevance order within the partition.** Phase 0 measured whether that partition
exists at the moment the score is decided. **In 73 of 74 sub-rank-1 pairs, it does
not.** The tactics are not too weak — they have nothing to act on.

This is a *precondition* failure, not a margin failure, which is why it is reported
as a result rather than as "below the noise floor". See §2 on why it generalises to
the private 800.

## 1. Why this was proposed — `public_0199`

Buying session, target `B089M57PSQ` (Boboking, boys' cotton briefs, Department
`Boys`), locked in at **turn 1, rank 2**. The winner was `B088QF5G58` — Hanes **men's**
briefs, 21,411 ratings against the target's 8,314.

Read against the **full constraint card** the target matches 4/4 and the winner 2/4,
and the card contains `size:3T (2-3), 4T (3-4), 5 (5-6)` — size vocabulary that
exists only in children's apparel. The session looks like a textbook audience
failure: a men's 6-pack surfaced against a toddler size run, winning on brand
popularity.

**That reading is wrong, and Phase 0 is what showed it.** At the lock-in turn the
customer had said only *"I'm looking for Underwear Briefs. A key requirement is:
cotton."* The size span was never disclosed — it is one of the 3 undisclosed spans
of 4. The 4/4-vs-2/4 comparison uses information the agent did not have.

## 2. The generalisation contract

Per He Jinhong: the submission is scored on a **private 800-session set**. A change
that fixes observed public sessions and nothing else is worth negative value, because
it consumes the one thing that does generalise — selection budget against 200
sessions that 16+ configs have already been fitted to (CLAUDE.md, "Measurement
discipline").

**The rule this PRD holds itself to**, taken from what has and has not generalised in
this repo's own record:

| generalised | why | did not generalise | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `per_field_depth` 220→1000 | structural cutoff; only ever admits genuinely-matching documents | `candidate_depth` 200→300 | admits whatever ranks next by raw score; dilutes |
| `constraint_commonness_penalty` | **measures** catalog document-frequency; built specifically to avoid a hardcoded phrase list | 7 learned rerankers | move weight among columns measuring the same thing |
| `span_all` | **adds information** the vector did not contain | — | — |
| synthetic span normalisation | reads the **evaluator's own construction** (`local_evaluator.py:57,61`) | — | — |

So every design below must be: **derived from catalog statistics or from the
evaluator's construction, with zero hand-written lists keyed to observed sessions,
and at most one fitted parameter, validated on fold B.**

**A hand-written `{3T, 4T, toddler, youth}` gazetteer written from `public_0199`
fails this contract outright** — and it is measurably not worth writing anyway:
**2 of 200** public cards contain any kid-audience or kid-size token, and **8 of 200**
targets are `boys`/`girls`/`kids` at all.

**Why the negative results below generalise.** They are not "the delta was inside
noise on 200 sessions." They are measurements of whether the mechanism's input
exists: live constraint spans tie in **74/74** pairs, structured verdicts tie in
**73/74**. That is a property of *how early the evaluator locks a session in*
(`best_rank` = first hit in top-10, break on first hit), not of which sessions were
drawn. Median disclosure at lock-in is **1 span of a 4-span card**; 27/74 pairs have
**zero** spans live. The private 800 is generated by the same simulator with the same
break rule, so the precondition fails there for the same structural reason.

## 3. Phase 0 — evidence (DONE)

**Method.** `tools/read_pairs.py` extended to snapshot, at each session's lock-in
turn, the live `Constraints`, the `evaluate_all` verdicts for target and winner, and
both products' `effective_gender`. Instrumented run reproduces
`TechnicalScore = 0.909328`, HR@10 = 1.000 exactly, so it is the real dialogue.
**74 sub-rank-1 pairs** (126 sessions at rank 1, 0 misses).
Read-only: `config/tuned.json` and `results.json` untouched (Critical rules 1, 2).

### 3.1 Does an audience partition exist? (T1)

| measurement | result |
|---|---|
| audience differs, target vs winner | **18/74** |
| ...of those, no gender constraint set at lock-in | 17 |
| ...of those, any kid-audience word said | **0** |
| gender constraint live at lock-in | 14/74 |
| ...target and winner **both** `SATISFIED` | **14/14** |
| winner already `VIOLATED` (−0.23) and still won | **0** |
| target itself wrongly `VIOLATED` | 0 |

**18/74 audience mismatches is a genuinely large bucket** — and every one of them is
adult men↔women, not kids. But where a gender constraint exists it never separates:
in all 14 cases both products satisfy it. **Raising `gender_violated` would change
nothing.**

### 3.2 Can audience be *derived* without the customer saying it? (T1)

The customer's opening line is built from `coarse_category(target.categories)`. So the
test is whether a **catalog-mined** level→Department distribution (all 50k rows, no
hand-written list) can name an audience and exclude the winner:

| confidence threshold | fires | names target's audience | **mis-names it** | **excludes winner** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.60 | 43/74 | 40 | **3** | **2** |
| 0.70 | 39/74 | 37 | 2 | 2 |
| 0.80 | 29/74 | 28 | 1 | 1 |
| 0.90 | 19/74 | 19 | 0 | **0** |

**Net at the most permissive setting: 2 helps against 3 harms, plus 40 firings that
change nothing but carry risk.** The mechanism inverts — it is confident exactly where
the category is already gendered (so the existing extractor already caught it) and
ambiguous exactly where the mismatch lives:

```
public_0068  target=women  winner=men    'Athletic Walking'          0.44  -> no call
public_0145  target=women  winner=men    'Active Athletic Socks'     0.46  -> no call
public_0164  target=women  winner=men    'Watches Watch Bands'       0.50  -> no call
public_0189  target=men    winner=women  'Shorts Denim'              0.45  -> no call
public_0199  target=boys   winner=men    'Underwear Briefs'          0.73  -> "men"  WRONG
```

**`public_0199` — the session that motivated the whole idea — infers `men` at 0.73,
because "Underwear Briefs" is 73% men's in this catalog.** The tactic would confidently
promote Hanes and demote the boys' target. It makes the originating session *worse*.

### 3.3 Is there anything for a facet-first sort to sort on? (T2)

| measurement | result |
|---|---|
| `evaluate_all` differs between target and winner at lock-in | **1/74** (one `material`) |
| live constraint-span hit counts differ | **0/74** — identical in every pair |
| median spans live at lock-in | **1**, of a median **4**-span card |
| pairs with zero spans live | 27/74 |

**T2's precondition fails in 73/74 pairs.** A lexicographic sort whose primary key is
constant across the two products it must separate is exactly the incumbent ordering.
The 4/4-vs-2/4 partition that motivated T2 is against the *full card*; against the
*live* spans it is 1/1 vs 1/1.

### 3.4 Is popularity crossing a category boundary? (T3)

| measurement | result |
|---|---|
| winner more popular than target | **28/74 (38%)** |
| ...**and** in a different leaf category | **1/74** |

Popularity is not systematically favouring the winner — 38% is on the wrong side of a
coin flip. And the cross-*cell* comparison T3 exists to correct occurs **once**.

**Cell sizes make a raw percentile unusable anyway** (all 50k rows):

| cell definition | distinct | median | cells with <5 members |
|---|---|---|---|
| category leaf | 796 | 11 | 34.2% |
| last-2 levels | 1,152 | 9 | 38.5% |
| leaf × audience | 1,962 | **4** | **53.8%** |

A percentile over 4 items is noise. Any T3 implementation must shrink toward the
global value, which is one fitted parameter.

**`public_0199` is itself a counter-example to T3.** Target and winner are both filed
under the same leaf, `Briefs` — so a within-cell percentile puts them in the *same*
cell and changes their relative order not at all. The Hanes/Boboking comparison looks
cross-department because of `details.Department`, but the category taxonomy this
catalog actually carries does not separate them.

### 3.5 A real correctness bug, found on the way

`check_gender` (`structured.py`) treats the kids hierarchy as flat:

```
check_gender(product[boys],   gender="kids") = violated   # wrong
check_gender(product[girls],  gender="kids") = violated   # wrong
check_gender(product[unisex], gender="kids") = violated   # wrong
```

A customer saying *"toddler"* or *"baby"* sets `constraints.gender = "kids"`
(`GENDER_WORDS`), and every boys'/girls'/unisex-child product then takes the full
**−0.23** `gender_violated` penalty. The reverse holds too: constraint `boys` against
a product filed `kids` is `VIOLATED` rather than `UNKNOWN`.

**0/200 was the wrong denominator.** Sizing it against the catalog instead of the
public sample: for all 50,000 rows, generate the opening line that row would produce
(`coarse_category` of its own category path), run the real extractor, and score the
row against the constraint its own listing caused —

**506 rows (1.01%) penalise themselves −0.23.** Two independent defects produce them:

| defect | rows | example |
|---|---|---|
| hierarchy: `kids` treated as a sibling of `boys`/`girls` | 313 | `'Baby Girls Bodysuits'` → `kids` vs filed `girls` |
| extraction: first gender word wins, so `baby` beats `girls` | 224 (subset) | `'Baby Boys Bodysuits'` → `kids`, discarding `boys` |
| genuine catalog mislabels — **not fixable here, left alone** | 180 | `'Women Gloves'` filed `men` |

At ~0.63% of rows the hierarchy path is ~1.3 expected sessions in 200 and ~5 in 800.
Observing 0/200 is consistent with chance, **not** with the bug being unreachable —
which is exactly the class of defect a public-200-only view cannot distinguish.

## 4. Tactic specifications

Each is specified in the form that satisfies §2, with a pre-registered kill criterion.

### T1a — kids hierarchy correctness fix · **BUILD**

**Change.** `structured.check_gender` only. Make the audience relation a partial order
instead of equality: `kids ⊇ {boys, girls, kids}`, and a product filed `kids` against a
constraint of `boys`/`girls` returns `UNKNOWN` (absence of specificity, not conflict) —
the same asymmetry the function already applies to `unisex` for adults.

**Parameters: zero.** Hand-written lists: zero (`GENDER_CANON` already exists and is
already catalog-derived).
**Risk:** monotone — it only ever converts a false `VIOLATED` into `SATISFIED`/`UNKNOWN`.
It cannot create a new penalty.
**Second change, same defect** (`ConstraintExtractor.update`): the gender scan took the
first match, so `"baby girls bodysuits"` resolved to `kids` and discarded the `girls`
beside it. Now a specific child audience outranks the generic one; first-match
semantics are preserved for every other pair.

**BUILT AND VERIFIED 2026-08-31.** Own-goal rate **506 → 180 rows (1.01% → 0.36%)**;
all 313 hierarchy rows cured, the 180 genuine mislabels correctly untouched. Full
unmodified CLI evaluator on all 200 public sessions: **0.909328, byte-identical** —
HR@10 1.000, MRR 0.756095, MTTC 1.875 all unchanged, which is the pre-registered kill
criterion passing rather than a null result. **51/51 tests** (49 + 2 new).
`results.json` untouched; every run went to a scratch path per Critical rule 1.

Adopted as a correctness fix, not a score play: its value is entirely on the private
800, and it is **unverifiable from the public set by construction**. Siblings still
conflict (`boys` vs `girls` → VIOLATED) and every adult rule is unchanged.

### T1b — catalog-derived audience inference · **DO NOT BUILD**

**Design (for the record).** Mine `category level → Department` from all 50k rows;
infer an audience when one dominates above a threshold; feed it as `constraints.gender`.
Contract-compliant: catalog-derived, one parameter.

**Killed at Phase 0 by §3.2.** Net 2 helps / 3 harms; inverts on the originating
session. Reopen only if a measurement shows the confident-and-correct region is larger
than the confident-and-wrong one — §3.2's table is the test, and it is already run.

### T2 — facet-first lexicographic ordering · **DO NOT BUILD**

**Design (for the record).** In `Ranker.rank`, replace the sort key
`(-score, parent_asin)` with `(-satisfied_facet_count, -score, parent_asin)`, bucketing
on `evaluate_all` outcomes and/or live span-match count. Zero parameters, structural.

**Killed at Phase 0 by §3.3.** The primary key is constant across the pair in 73/74
cases; span hits tie in 74/74. **Conditional on T1b or another facet producing a
partition — and T1b is killed.** Reopen only when a change makes §3.3's first two rows
non-trivial; that measurement re-runs in ~3 minutes.

**Additional risk if it is ever revived:** a lexicographic sort is discontinuous. One
noisy constraint extraction moves a product a whole bucket, and CLAUDE.md's own
`candidate_depth` finding is that ordering changes which help targeted sessions
routinely cost MRR elsewhere. Fold B, not mean MRR, is the gate.

### T3 — within-cell popularity · **OPTIONAL, LOW EXPECTED VALUE**

The only tactic Phase 0 does not structurally rule out, because it does not require a
target/winner partition — it rescales an existing signal. `w_log_rating_number = 0.88`
is one of the largest weights in the config, so the signal it rescales is load-bearing.

**Change.**
1. `catalog._fit_priors`: compute a shrunk within-cell popularity alongside the global
   one. Cell = category leaf (median 11 members; **not** leaf×audience, median 4).
   Shrink toward the global value with the same Bayesian form already used for
   `quality`: `rel = (cell_pct · n + global_pct · k) / (n + k)`, `k` the one fitted
   parameter.
2. `features.py`: add `popularity_relative` as a **new** column. `FEATURE_NAMES`
   49 → 50.
3. `config.py`: `w_popularity_relative: float = 0.0`.

**Default 0.0 makes the incumbent byte-identical** — the pattern `span_all` and
`w_title_low_coverage` already follow, and the mandatory gate before any sweep.

**Parameters: two** (shrinkage `k`, weight). That is the most of any tactic here and
the reason it ranks last despite being the only survivor.

**Expected value, stated honestly: near zero on the public set.** §3.4 measures the
cross-cell comparison it corrects occurring **1/74** times. It is worth running only
as a cheap test of whether the *global* popularity scale is miscalibrated in a way the
public set cannot show — not because the pairs evidence supports it.

**Kill criteria (pre-registered, adopt only if all three hold):**
- fold B ≥ 0 at seed 7 — the gate `candidate_depth` failed and `per_field_depth` passed;
- HR@10 stays 1.000 — no regression is acceptable now that every session hits;
- response monotone and saturating across `k`, not a single-point peak (the
  `constraint_commonness_penalty` signature; a jagged optimum is selection-on-noise).

**Interaction to watch:** `w_popularity_low_coverage = -0.4` already conditions
popularity on query coverage. T3 conditions it on cell membership. These may be
substitutes — measure T3 against the live config, and if it wins, re-check whether
`w_popularity_low_coverage` is still earning its weight.

## 4b. T4 — brand false positives: a real defect that is score-inert

**Found by generalising T1a's own-goal test to all six constraint dimensions** —
evaluate each of the 200 public targets against constraints drawn from its own card:

| dimension | targets VIOLATED by their own listing |
|---|---|
| **brand** | **114 / 200** |
| gender / category / price | 1 each |
| material / color | 0 |

`BrandVocabulary` matches single ordinary words that are also store names somewhere
in a 19,855-brand catalog. The offenders are exactly the boilerplate the simulator
quotes most. **Measured live at the lock-in turn across all 200 sessions: a brand was
extracted in 66 sessions and 62 were wrong — a 94% false-positive rate**, driven by
`wash` (20), `sole` (15), `hand` (15), `machine` (11), out of *"Machine Wash"* and
*"Rubber sole"*. The hand-written `BRAND_BLOCKLIST` covers `fashion`/`premium`/`gap`
and misses every one of these.

**The measured gate.** Ordinary words and real brands separate by two orders of
magnitude on text document-frequency, so no curation is needed:

```
sole 0.206   wash 0.317   hand 0.168   machine 0.214    <- all spurious
hanes 0.0021  crocs 0.0030  carhartt 0.0014  skechers 0.0077   <- all real
```

Threshold **0.01** catches 14 of 15 observed offenders, keeps all 13 real brands
tested, and drops 0.8% of the single-word vocabulary. (`totes`, 0.0077, is the one
survivor — and genuinely ambiguous, since Totes is a real brand.)

**Implemented and verified: `RetrievalConfig.brand_max_text_commonness`, applied at
match time.** Build-time filtering would have been a silent no-op, because
`Agent.apply_config` deliberately does not rebuild the extractor — the same trap
`rerank_depth` set. Default 0.0 reproduces the incumbent byte-identically.

### The result: correct, and score-inert. **0.909328 at every threshold tested.**

| threshold | fold A | HR@10 | MRR | MTTC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 (off) | 0.902707 | 1.0000 | 0.737690 | 1.930 |
| 0.005 … 0.20 | **0.902707** (identical) | 1.0000 | 0.737690 | 1.930 |

Full 200 at 0.01: **0.909328, HR@10 1.000, MRR 0.756095, MTTC 1.875** — byte-identical
to the incumbent.

**Why, and this is the part worth keeping.** `check_brand` returns VIOLATED for every
product whose `brand_key` is not the extracted one. A spurious brand therefore applies
−0.06 to ~99.4% of the pool **uniformly**, and a uniform offset cannot reorder
anything. Only two classes of candidate differ:

- **8 products in 50,000 (0.016%)** are literally stored as `Sole`/`Wash`/`Hand`/
  `Machine`/`Work`/`Pants`/`Easy` and would wrongly take `brand_satisfied` **+0.18**.
  A 200-candidate pool draws one essentially never.
- **314 rows (0.63%)** have no store at all and score UNKNOWN (0.0) rather than −0.06,
  a small relative advantage over every branded row.

**Correction to the claim that motivated this work.** It was reported mid-session that
a spurious match produces "a 0.24 swing in the wrong direction" (target −0.06 vs
imposter +0.18). That is arithmetically right but practically wrong: it requires the
literally-named product to be in the pool, which the 0.016% figure above rules out.
**The false-positive rate is real and high; the scoring consequence is not.**

**Status: implemented, verified byte-identical, adoption is a judgement call.**
Enabling it at 0.01 removes a 94%-wrong signal and the two residual asymmetries above
for exactly 0.000000 on the public set — the same correctness-insurance argument as
T1a, and the same inability of this set to verify it. Reverting it is equally
defensible on the grounds that it is ~40 lines buying no measured score.
`config/tuned.json` was NOT edited; the sweep ran against scratch copies throughout.

## 6. What the evidence points at instead

Phase 0 reproduces, from a third independent direction, the finding CLAUDE.md already
records twice: **median 1 span live out of a 4-span card at lock-in, 27/74 pairs with
zero spans.** Every tactic in this PRD needs a facet the customer has not yet named.

That is the same wall as the roadmap's top item — **confidence-gated withholding /
ask-before-recommend**, ceiling measured at **0.9544 (hard constraints) to 0.9638
(full card)** after the synthetic-span fix. The arithmetic there is unchanged: one
extra turn costs ~0.0001 per session via MTTC, a rank 2→1 pays 0.00075 — **7.5×**.

**The honest reframing of He Jinhong's constraint** (*"without asking even more
questions"*): the measurement says the information needed to separate these pairs is
not present at lock-in under any feature. There is no facet to exploit because the
customer has not spoken yet. The 74 pairs classify **A=0, B=65, C=9** — zero ranking
defects, 65 timing defects, 9 structural ties. A no-questions solution is bounded by
the 0 in that first column.
